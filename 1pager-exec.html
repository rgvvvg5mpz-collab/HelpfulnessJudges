01 / The problem

Our assistants answer questions about people’s money at the worst moments of their lives — job loss, a market drop, a death in the family.

02 / The solution — one turn, ten scores

Not one “helpfulness” score — a single number hides which thing broke. Every judge scores every turn; none can suppress another.

What a reviewer sees reads fine

Customer

“I got laid off Friday. What happens to my 401(k)?”

Assistant

“I’m so sorry — that sounds really stressful. Generally you have a few options: leave it, roll it over, or withdraw, though penalties may apply. I’ve pulled up your account for you.”

Warm. Accurate. Signed off in a skim.

Bright lines force a zero regardless of everything else. 660 labelled samples test the judges.

03 / The impact

A number, not an opinion

Comparable across releases, and across assistants.

Status — read this before funding it

No judge has scored a real customer transcript. Zero. No agreement figure against human raters exists. The 660 samples are model-generated with an independent verifier; 68 contested.
