The decision everything follows from

why a pre-rendered attribute, not Arize’s own assembly

SPAN scope + judge.transcript — chosen

Every judge, including the two conversation-level ones, reads one attribute your instrumentation writes. The transcript format the rubrics reference by name survives character-for-character, and the whole thing is scriptable through flat column_mappings.

Trace / session scope — rejected

column_mappings is a flat dict with no subquery binding, so multi-span mapping is UI-only and unscriptable. Session scope also writes one result to the root span of the session’s first trace and truncates at 100,000 chars — so a verbose early turn silently eats the budget and the latest turns vanish.

The limitation that decides whether this path is acceptable. Arize’s InvocationParamsRequest carries reasoning controls for Gemini and OpenAI and nothing for Anthropic. The suite runs every judge at effort: high. There is no way to request it here — which is why ../agentcore-integration exists.
